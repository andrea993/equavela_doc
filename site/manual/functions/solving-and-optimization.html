    Functions

    Solving and optimization

    find maximize minerr minimize polyroots root

    find

    find(x, y, …)

    Solve block: values of the unknowns that satisfy the constraints of the Given block above.

    Notes
    Levenberg–Marquardt on the residuals of the equations and the violations of the inequalities, starting from the guess values; succeeds only when every constraint holds within CTOL. With one unknown it returns its value, with several a column vector in call order. find(x, y) → solves symbolically. May be written Find.

    maximize

    maximize(f, x, …)

    Solve block: the values of the unknowns that maximise f under the constraints.

    Notes
    As minimize, for the largest value.

    minerr

    minerr(x, y, …)

    Solve block: the closest approximation when the constraints cannot all be met.

    Notes
    The same algorithm as find, returning the best point found even when the constraints are not met; check the result.

    minimize

    minimize(f, x, …)

    Solve block: the values of the unknowns that minimise f under the constraints.

    Notes
    A quadratic penalty method (weights 10, 10³, 10⁵, 10⁷), each stage minimised by BFGS with central-difference gradients. f is named without arguments. The final point must satisfy the constraints within CTOL. Compare Optim.optimize in Julia.

    polyroots

    polyroots(v)

    Roots of the polynomial with coefficients v (constant term first, 2 <= length <= 99).

    Julia
    LinearAlgebra.eigvals
    Notes
    All roots of the polynomial with the coefficients v, constant term first (2 to 99 coefficients, the last non-zero), as the eigenvalues of the companion matrix. Real roots come back as real numbers. Equivalent to Polynomials.roots(Polynomial(v)) in Julia.

    root

    root(f(x), x) / root(f(x), x, a, b)

    A root of f in the variable x, from the guess value of x or on the interval [a, b].

    Notes
    Without an interval, the secant method from the guess (the current value of x; a complex guess finds complex roots), accepting the point only if |f(x)| ≤ TOL. With an interval [a, b] where f changes sign, bisection to machine precision. Units are allowed: the result has the unit of the guess or of the bounds. The first argument is an expression of the variable, not a function value, so root(x² − 2, x) works directly. Compare Roots.find_zero in Julia. A worksheet definition named root takes precedence; the n-th root is the operator ⁿ√x.
